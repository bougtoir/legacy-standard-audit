# Phase R2 handoff

## Central claim decision

The original sentence is supportable only as a bounded governance proposition and is nearly tautological without the operational protocol. The paper will not claim a new theory of lock-in, standards review, regret, or switching costs.

## Surviving novelty

The novelty claim is the reproducible integration of:

- a frozen heterogeneous case design;
- capability-specific re-audit triggers;
- incumbent/current/transition benchmark separation;
- retained null and insufficient-evidence outcomes;
- a prohibition on converting static technical gains directly into redesign recommendations.

## Evidence ceiling

The cases demonstrate protocol portability and heterogeneous classifications. They do not estimate prevalence, a pooled effect, realized deployment gains, historical optimality, or transition-adjusted superiority.

## Carried to R3

R3 must determine whether any primary case has Level A transition evidence. If none does, the absence of an estimate becomes a substantive decision-gate result rather than a gap to fill speculatively.
