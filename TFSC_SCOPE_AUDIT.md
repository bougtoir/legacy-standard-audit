# TFSC scope audit

## Technology centrality test

The paper is in scope only if its principal contribution concerns how
technological change alters feasible control, information, coordination, or
optimization conditions while incumbent standards persist. A collection of
historical curiosities, ergonomic mismatches, or unrelated operations-research
examples would not satisfy this test.

## Intended contribution

1. A formal distinction among historical, current frictionless, and
   transition-adjusted optima.
2. A reproducible audit protocol linking technological transitions to
   measurable mismatch or justified persistence.
3. Cross-domain evidence on when cheap sensing, computation, communication,
   automation, and digital coordination make fixed standards reassessable.
4. Governance rules that explicitly preserve null findings and cases where
   transition costs rationally support the incumbent.

## Desk-rejection risks to audit

- technology appears only as context;
- cases are incomparable or selected for striking effects;
- the paper reproduces path-dependence or standards-economics arguments without
  a new framework or empirical contribution;
- switching and network costs are omitted;
- historical rationales are asserted rather than documented;
- policy recommendations exceed narrow engineering objectives;
- TFSC literature and current author requirements are not engaged.

## Current status

Official TFSC requirements and DOI-verified prior art were reviewed in Phases
1–2. The project has a defensible provisional fit with the Technology
Governance and Public Policy bureau and secondary fit with Impact and Evaluation
of Technology. Final scope remains conditional on frozen cases showing that
specific technologies materially changed information, control, computation,
coordination, or feasible-set constraints.
