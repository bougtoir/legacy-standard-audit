# TFSC positioning statement

Technologies often change the information, control, computation, and
coordination conditions under which standards operate, while installed bases,
regulation, compatibility, and organizational routines slow revision. Existing
research explains standards, lock-in, technological trajectories, and
socio-technical transitions, but it does not provide a common falsifiable audit
that reconstructs the standard's original decision problem and separately
estimates contemporary frictionless and transition-adjusted optima across
heterogeneous domains. This study develops and applies that audit without
presuming that age implies obsolescence. Its contribution is a reproducible
measurement and governance protocol: pre-specified case selection, explicit
technology mechanisms, uncertainty, switching and network costs, and mandatory
null controls. The intended TFSC contribution is to show when fixed standards
should remain, adapt, or be reconsidered as technological constraints change.
