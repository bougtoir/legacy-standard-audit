# TFSC submission requirements

Verified against the official *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*
(TFSC) Guide for Authors and journal page on 2026-09-24. Recheck all
requirements immediately before submission because Elsevier may revise them.

## Scope and editorial positioning

- TFSC publishes research on technology and its impact on society.
- The journal currently routes submissions among four bureaus:
  Technological Forecasting and Modelling, Management of Technology, Impact and
  Evaluation of Technology, and Technology Governance and Public Policy.
- Social change alone is out of scope unless examined through specific
  technological developments.
- A publishable paper must make substantial theoretical and practical or
  policy contributions, use a current and internally consistent theoretical
  basis, and provide methodological development.
- The present project should primarily target **Technology Governance and
  Public Policy**, with a secondary fit to **Impact and Evaluation of
  Technology**. The manuscript must make technological constraint change
  central rather than treating technology as background context.

## Article type and review

- Eligible formats include full scholarly articles and short research notes
  describing significant work in progress or posing research problems.
- The intended submission is a full research article.
- Review is double anonymized and normally uses at least two independent
  reviewers after editorial screening.
- Submit a title page and an anonymized manuscript as separate files.

## Length and structure

- The current guide does not state a general full-manuscript word limit.
- Abstract: no more than 250 words; factual and self-contained; state purpose,
  procedures, principal findings, significance where applicable, and
  conclusions.
- Keywords: 1–7, in English.
- Sections and subsections must be clearly numbered; the abstract is unnumbered.
- Mathematical expressions must remain editable and displayed equations must
  be numbered in citation order.
- Each author biography is limited to 100 words.

## Required submission files

1. Editable title page (`.doc` or `.docx`) containing:
   - concise title;
   - author names in submission-system order;
   - full affiliations and countries;
   - corresponding-author full postal address and email;
   - present/permanent addresses when applicable;
   - acknowledgements;
   - competing-interest declaration unless supplied separately.
2. Editable anonymized manuscript (`.doc`, `.docx`, or `.tex`) containing the
   main text, references, and tables, with no author-identifying information.
3. Highlights as a separate editable file, named to include “highlights”:
   3–5 bullets, each no more than 85 characters including spaces.
4. Each figure as a separate, logically named file.
5. Supplementary files, if any, submitted concurrently and cited in the text.
6. A separate editable competing-interest declaration produced through the
   Elsevier declarations tool.
7. A graphical abstract is encouraged but not required; if supplied, submit it
   separately at 531 × 1328 pixels (height × width) or proportionally larger,
   readable at 5 × 13 cm, preferably TIFF, EPS, PDF, or Microsoft Office.

A PDF is not an acceptable manuscript source file. Word files should use a
single-column layout.

## Tables, figures, and supplementary material

- Tables must be editable text rather than images, numbered by first
  appearance, captioned, cited in the text, and free of vertical rules and
  unnecessary shading.
- Figures must be numbered and cited by first appearance, have captions, and
  be supplied separately. Vector drawings should be EPS or PDF. Photographs
  require at least 300 dpi, line drawings 1000 dpi, charts/large shaded tables
  may be XLS/XLSX, and annotated images may be PPT/PPTX.
- All figures must remain accurate and reproducible from underlying data.
- Supplementary files must be relevant, accurately captioned, cited in the
  manuscript, and supplied at initial submission. They will be published as
  received without production formatting.

## Research data and code

TFSC applies Elsevier research-data policy Option C:

- deposit research data in a relevant repository;
- cite and link the dataset in the article; or
- state why sharing is impossible.

A data-availability statement is required at submission. For this project, the
submission package must link the public code, distributable source data,
derived data, model definitions, and reproducibility instructions. Restricted
or non-redistributable raw materials must be represented by provenance and
retrieval metadata rather than uploaded.

## References

- Submission formatting is flexible if internally consistent.
- TFSC's stated citation style is author–year, not numbered: single author,
  both authors for two-author works, and first author plus “et al.” for three
  or more authors.
- The reference list is alphabetical, then chronological for repeated authors.
- Every in-text citation must have a corresponding reference and vice versa.
- DOI links should be included where available.
- Datasets and software should be cited as research outputs; dataset references
  use a `[dataset]` marker before the reference.
- Every reference must resolve to a real source. Preprints must be identified
  clearly and replaced by peer-reviewed versions when available.

## Declarations requiring human-author confirmation

Do not infer or invent any of the following:

- final author list, order, affiliations, ORCIDs, and corresponding author;
- author approval of the submitted version;
- CRediT roles;
- funding sources and sponsor roles, or confirmation of no specific funding;
- financial and personal competing interests;
- acknowledgements;
- permission for third-party copyrighted material;
- decision on subscription publication versus open access;
- final data/code repository and release DOI.

The corresponding author must be designated before submission. TFSC generally
does not consider authorship changes after submission and disallows them after
acceptance.

## Generative-AI disclosure

Substantive generative-AI assistance must be disclosed in a section immediately
before the references. AI cannot be listed as an author. Human authors remain
responsible for verifying accuracy, sources, originality, privacy, intellectual
property, and the final interpretation.

Provisional disclosure, requiring human-author review:

> During the preparation of this work the author(s) used Devin, an AI-assisted
> software engineering and research environment, to support literature
> retrieval, reproducible code development, document drafting, and quality
> checks. The author(s) reviewed and edited all outputs, verified cited sources
> and numerical results, and take full responsibility for the content of the
> published article.

Generative AI must not fabricate or alter primary observed data. Reproducible
data visualizations and explanatory schematics are permitted, with disclosure
in captions when generative AI was used to create the image.

## Publishing model and current journal indicators

As displayed on the official journal page on 2026-09-24:

- subscription publication charges no publication fee to authors;
- optional open access listed an APC of USD 4,750 excluding taxes, subject to
  reductions during submission;
- CiteScore: 30.5;
- Impact Factor: 13.5;
- median journal-page indicators: 16 days to first decision, 126 days to
  decision after review, 337 days to acceptance, and 11 days from acceptance to
  online publication.

These values are journal-page context only and must not be represented as
guarantees or used as empirical manuscript evidence.

## Final submission gate

Before upload, confirm:

- full contact details for the corresponding author;
- all editable source files, artwork, captions, tables, footnotes,
  supplementary materials, and highlights are present;
- anonymization covers the manuscript and supplement;
- spelling and grammar checks are complete;
- citations and references are bidirectionally complete;
- permissions exist for all third-party material;
- data statement and repository links resolve;
- funding, competing interests, CRediT, acknowledgements, and AI disclosure
  have explicit human-author approval;
- all highlighted claims remain supported by the released data and code.
