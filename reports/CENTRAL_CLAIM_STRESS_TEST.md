# Central claim stress test

## Claim tested

> Technological change can make a fixed standard reassessable without making it obsolete.

## Hostile assessment

### Is it supported?

**As a bounded governance proposition, yes.**

The frozen cases show that a technology-mediated capability change can produce different audit outcomes: material oracle-benchmark mismatch, small or uncertain mismatch, no mismatch under the stated objective, and insufficient evidence. This supports reassessment without a presumption of replacement.

### Is it tautological?

**In its current sentence form, nearly.**

Saying that change can justify reassessment is weak unless the paper specifies:

- what capability changed;
- which objective and feasible set are compared;
- what evidence counts as static mismatch;
- what prevents a static gain from becoming a redesign recommendation;
- what outcome labels are possible when evidence is null or incomplete.

The contribution is therefore not the sentence itself. It is the reproducible audit procedure and its evidence gates.

### Is it too broad or too weak?

**Too broad if “technological change” is unqualified; too weak if left as a general maxim.**

The relevant change is an observable, case-linked alteration in sensing, prediction, computation, communication, metering, coordination, or control that expands or changes the feasible decision set. The protocol does not cover every kind of technological change or every standard.

### Is it oracle-dependent?

**The strongest empirical classifications are.**

`WK07` compares fixed replacement with a perfect remaining-life oracle in simulated C-MAPSS data. `UI09` compares calendar application with a perfect-weather/model reference-crop benchmark. Removing either reduces the positive material examples; removing both eliminates material primary-case mismatch. This dependency must be a central result, not a footnote.

### Is it strongest-case-dependent?

**The claim that the protocol yields heterogeneous audit states is not; any claim of demonstrated widespread material mismatch is.**

The null and insufficient-evidence cases support the anti-presumption design. They do not support a claim that legacy standards are commonly inefficient. The empirical demonstration is that the protocol can return different outcomes, not that it estimates a population prevalence.

### Is it already covered by prior theory?

**Its components are.**

Prior literature already covers:

- path dependence, lock-in, installed-base effects, and increasing returns;
- standards as institutions that interact with innovation;
- socio-technical transitions and technology governance;
- technology adoption or replacement under switching costs and network effects;
- static, dynamic, and switching regret in decision and online-optimization settings;
- periodic confirmation, revision, or withdrawal of standards through ISO systematic review;
- structured standards analysis across lifecycle and implementation perspectives, including NIST FACTS.

The present work should not claim invention of lock-in theory, standards review, regret, or transition-cost reasoning.

## Targeted antecedent comparison

| Antecedent | What it already supplies | Remaining distinction of this project |
|---|---|---|
| Path dependence and lock-in | Persistence, increasing returns, installed-base and coordination mechanisms | A case-auditable comparison that does not infer inefficiency from persistence |
| Standards and innovation literature | Co-evolution, dominance, diffusion, compatibility, and institutional roles | A frozen heterogeneous case protocol with explicit null and insufficient-evidence retention |
| ISO systematic review | Periodic decisions to confirm, revise/amend, convert, or withdraw | An evidence model linking a specified capability change to static and transition gates |
| NIST FACTS | Structured analysis, comparison, testing, lifecycle, stakeholders, and implementation views | Objective/feasible-set reconstruction plus generated static evidence and a redesign evidence ceiling |
| Switching-cost/adoption models | Net utility, irreversibility, network effects, and transition costs | A rule prohibiting static technical gains from being relabeled as redesign evidence |
| Regret literature | Formal comparison with fixed or changing comparators and switching penalties | A domain-portable audit vocabulary; not a new regret theorem or online-learning result |
| Evaluation and evidence frameworks | Pre-specification, sensitivity analysis, null retention, and evidence grading | Their integration into audits of heterogeneous legacy standards after capability change |

No single located antecedent was found to combine all of the following in one reproducible empirical protocol:

1. a pre-specified candidate universe and frozen heterogeneous cases;
2. explicit separation of incumbent, current frictionless, and transition-adjusted benchmarks;
3. retained null and insufficient-evidence controls;
4. case-specific objectives with no pooled magnitude;
5. an evidence gate preventing static technical advantage from automatically authorizing redesign.

This is a bounded combination claim, not proof that no closer antecedent exists.

## Surviving central claim

The strongest defensible formulation is:

> This study does not propose a new theory of lock-in. It provides a reproducible audit protocol for deciding when a documented change in technological capability warrants reassessment of a legacy standard, while preventing a static technical advantage from being treated automatically as evidence for redesign.

## Empirical claim ceiling

The seven frozen cases demonstrate protocol operation across heterogeneous objectives and evidence states. They do not identify:

- the prevalence of inefficient legacy standards;
- a common effect size;
- realized deployment benefits;
- a fully reconstructed historical optimum;
- transition-adjusted superiority unless case-compatible implementation evidence supports it.

## Falsifiers

The methodological contribution would be materially weakened if:

- case selection used observed effect direction or narrative usefulness;
- null or insufficient-evidence cases were removed;
- outputs depended on unregistered data;
- the same evidence threshold could not be applied without changing case-specific objectives;
- transition costs were rhetorically acknowledged but did not affect recommendations;
- conclusions of material mismatch survived only by calling oracle bounds realized effects.

## Revision decision

Use the surviving formulation as the conceptual anchor. Replace general claims about obsolete optimization problems with claims about documented capability changes, current benchmark evidence, and transition-evidence gates. Present periodic re-audit as a governance response; reserve redesign for cases that satisfy both static and transition requirements.
