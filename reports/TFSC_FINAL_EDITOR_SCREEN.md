# TFSC final editor screen

## Verdict before final revision

**Potentially in scope, but at substantial desk-rejection risk without reframing.**

The manuscript has a clear technological focus: sensing, computation, communication, prognostics, interval metering, and adaptive control change what can be observed or acted upon. Its strongest TFSC fit is a methodological contribution to the **Impact and Evaluation of Technology** bureau, with a secondary **Technology Governance and Public Policy** contribution. The present framing nevertheless risks being read as seven unrelated technical examples attached to an overbroad obsolescence claim.

## Current official requirements used

The current official TFSC guide states that submissions must focus on technology and its impact on society, make substantial theoretical and practical contributions, use a current and consistent theoretical basis, and include methodological development. It explicitly includes evaluation of technological effects and development of technology-governance processes. The journal uses double-anonymized review.

Relevant submission requirements include:

- editable, single-column source files;
- a separate title page and anonymized manuscript;
- abstract no longer than 250 words;
- one to seven English keywords;
- three to five highlights, each no longer than 85 characters including spaces;
- all tables cited and editable;
- all figures cited and supplied separately;
- supplementary files cited and submitted with the manuscript;
- mandatory research-data deposit/linking or an explanation of why sharing is not possible;
- a data-availability statement;
- a generative-AI declaration when applicable;
- funding, competing-interest, and CRediT information;
- real and complete references, with DOI inclusion where available.

## Desk-screen questions

### Is technology central?

**Yes, if the argument is rewritten around changed technological capability.**

The cases concern capabilities that alter observation, prediction, coordination, or control. The paper should not be positioned as a general critique of old standards. Its question is narrower: when does a specific technological capability make the incumbent decision problem worth re-auditing?

### Is the contribution TFSC-relevant?

**Yes, but mainly as a method and governance protocol.**

The defensible contribution is a reproducible sequence that:

1. states an incumbent decision problem without assuming historical optimality;
2. identifies the technology-mediated capability change;
3. compares the incumbent with a current benchmark;
4. treats implementation and switching evidence as a separate gate;
5. retains null and insufficient-evidence outcomes;
6. produces a re-audit classification rather than an automatic redesign recommendation.

This is relevant to technology evaluation and adaptive governance. It is not a new general theory of lock-in and not evidence of a universal obsolete-standard effect.

### Does the manuscript look like seven unrelated examples?

**Currently, partly.**

The common equations and frozen protocol help, but the results presentation foregrounds incomparable effect magnitudes. The final version should organize synthesis around common audit states:

- technology mechanism;
- static-evidence status;
- transition-evidence level;
- comparator type;
- final classification.

Case-specific magnitudes should remain within case subsections. No pooled effect should be implied.

### Is the governance contribution explicit?

**Not yet explicit enough.**

The governance rule should be:

> A technological capability change can trigger a structured re-audit. Redesign requires additional evidence that the preferred feasible alternative remains favorable after implementation, switching, compatibility, safety, and distributional constraints.

This separates periodic review from automatic sunset or replacement.

### Does the abstract respect the evidence ceiling?

**Mostly, but it needs stronger qualification.**

The revised abstract must say that:

- the two material primary results are oracle-benchmark results;
- effects are not pooled;
- transition evidence does not support forced point estimates unless R3 finds Level A evidence;
- the protocol distinguishes reassessment from redesign.

### Does the title overpromise?

**Yes.**

“Frozen solutions to obsolete optimization problems” presumes both a demonstrated historical solution and an obsolete problem. Neither is identified consistently across cases. The title should instead foreground re-auditing after technological change and the pre-specified protocol.

Recommended working title:

> Re-auditing legacy standards after technological change: a pre-specified framework for static and transition-adjusted evidence

### Is a re-audit trigger distinct from a redesign recommendation?

**Conceptually yes, rhetorically not yet enough.**

The distinction must appear in the title/abstract logic, end of the Introduction, audit framework, Discussion opening, practical implications, and Conclusion.

### Is literature integration sufficient?

**Broad but not fully integrated.**

The existing literature establishes path dependence, standards and innovation, socio-technical transitions, digital capability, and switching behavior. The final paper must also distinguish the protocol from:

- ISO's periodic systematic-review process;
- standards lifecycle and implementation-analysis frameworks such as NIST FACTS;
- status-quo choice with switching costs;
- online-learning uses of static/dynamic/switching regret;
- technology adoption and replacement models.

The novelty claim should be the integration and operational audit sequence, not ownership of any component concept.

## Editorial recommendation

Proceed with revision rather than abandoning the submission. The paper becomes reviewable if it consistently presents one claim: changed technological capability is a reason to **audit**, while a redesign recommendation requires a separate transition-evidence gate. Failure to narrow the title, discipline oracle language, or expose the transition-evidence ceiling would justify desk rejection.
