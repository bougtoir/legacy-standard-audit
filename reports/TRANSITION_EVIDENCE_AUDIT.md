# Transition Evidence Audit

## Decision

No primary case reaches Level A. The transition analysis is therefore an evidence gate, not a transition-adjusted effectiveness estimate. The credible sources identify implementation categories and plausible magnitudes in other settings, but none supplies case-compatible equipment, installation, commissioning, maintenance, control, communication, error, operational, useful-life, safety, regulatory, and valuation inputs for the exact benchmark analyzed here.

The incumbent cannot be judged transition-adjusted inefficient from the current evidence. The defensible output is the maximum transition burden that each frictionless benchmark could absorb in its native units, together with the measurements required to convert that ceiling into a decision.

## Evidence rule

- **Level A:** case-compatible empirical transition-cost evidence; a transition-adjusted estimate may be calculated.
- **Level B:** credible but insufficiently case-specific evidence; only a threshold or break-even gate is permitted.
- **Level C:** weak, duplicate, provenance-only, or non-comparable evidence; no transition-cost inference is permitted.

The machine-readable source-level assessment is `outputs/tables/transition_evidence_matrix.csv`. It contains 0 Level A, 11 Level B, and 2 Level C records. Raw snapshots, retrieval metadata, sizes, hashes, terms, and completeness are registered in `SOURCE_REGISTRY.csv`; raw originals remain under the Git-excluded `data/raw/transition_evidence/` directory.

## BE03 — occupancy-responsive lighting

### Evidence

`TR-BE03-DOE-GUIDE` is Level B. The DOE FEMP guide documents substantial variation by room type, a university deployment covering more than 200 rooms with approximately USD 14,000 annual savings and a 4.2-year simple payback, reduced wiring labor for wireless sensors, typical PIR battery life of 6–12 years, and dependence on coverage, geometry, schedules, timeout, temperature, and installation conditions.

`TR-BE03-GSA-FINDINGS` is Level B. The GSA/LBNL field demonstrations show that installation and commissioning complexity constrain adoption, wireless control avoids dedicated control wiring, payback depends strongly on the baseline, and unfamiliarity can increase labor. The reported 3–6-year payback and approximately USD 1/ft² illustrative incremental cost belong to the assessed federal sites and assumptions.

### Gate

The BE03 data identify 1,766 fewer nominal illuminated minute-observations under perfect occupancy information. If observations are one minute, the gross ceiling is:

\[
B_{\mathrm{BE03}}=1766\,\mathrm{min}\times \frac{P_{\mathrm{controlled}}}{60}
=29.433\,P_{\mathrm{controlled}}\ \mathrm{kWh}.
\]

A monetary gate additionally requires an applicable energy price and any demand-charge treatment. Sensor false positives, false negatives, occupied-lighting penalties, installation, commissioning, communication, maintenance, and battery replacement must be charged against the same ceiling. The DOE and GSA values cannot be substituted for those missing inputs.

## WK07 — condition-informed maintenance

### Evidence

`TR-WK07-NASA-REPORT` is Level B. It decomposes adoption cost into model and algorithm development, sensors and data collection, calibration and maintenance, legacy-system integration, skilled labor, validation, certification, secure infrastructure, and operational costs. It also identifies data quality, reliability, safety assurance, and regulatory approval as binding constraints.

`TR-WK07-FAA-AC` is Level B. The advisory circular requires attention to sensor and transmission performance, security, data retention, reliability, maintenance-program integration, operational authorization, and airworthiness decisions.

The corresponding NASA and FAA landing pages are Level C because they verify identity and version but add no independent transition input.

### Gate

The simulated oracle gap is 0.002499 preventive-replacement-cost units per operating cycle. A recurring implementation burden must be smaller than that rate. A one-time cost \(C_0\) is not comparable until a fleet exposure \(N\), useful life \(T\), discount rule, and recurring burden \(C_{\mathrm{op}}\) are identified:

\[
\frac{C_0+\operatorname{PV}(C_{\mathrm{op}})}{N}
< 0.002499.
\]

No observed fleet savings, safety improvement, or authorization benefit is inferred.

## UI01 — bounded price-aware load shifting

### Evidence

`TR-UI01-DOE-AMI` is Level B. Across SGIG projects, reported AMI deployment costs ranged from approximately USD 130 to USD 1,895 per meter, with communications, data management, workforce training, customer outreach, cybersecurity, interoperability, density, geography, scope, and prior utility experience affecting cost.

`TR-UI01-JRC-CBA` is Level B. It requires local boundary conditions, implementation choices, time horizon, discounting, cost and benefit identification, sensitivity analysis, and explicit treatment of externalities that may not be monetizable.

`TR-UI01-ACER` is Level B. It identifies suitable smart-meter functionality, near-real-time data, interoperable ICT services, appropriate contracts, and customer participation as enabling conditions for demand response.

### Gate

The fixed-price 1% shifting calculation supports a ceiling of EUR 0.071845 per baseline MWh, or EUR 14.629 per shifted MWh. This is not a permissible AMI budget: the dataset is an aggregate market series and has no compatible meter count, customer population, contract, or implementation horizon. It is the maximum combined incremental burden that could be tolerated if the modeled load movement, fixed prices, and all other assumptions were realized.

## UI09 — weather-adaptive irrigation

### Evidence

`TR-UI09-NRCS-449` is Level B. The standard recognizes crop ET with crop coefficients, soil-moisture monitoring, computerized scheduling using real-time climate, soil, and crop information, telemetry, flow measurement, application uniformity, soil water-holding capacity, crop stage, and irrigation-system characteristics.

`TR-UI09-USU-PAGE` is Level B. Its agricultural monitoring package includes three water-content sensors, one matric-potential sensor, a data logger, five years of telemetry, and an approximate USD 4,500 total value. The package is not the UI09 intervention and supplies no compatible outcome model.

`TR-UI09-UCANR` is Level B. The urban-landscape program evaluation documents installation, programming, maintenance, signal fees, system design, fine tuning, water rates, and expected life. Its illustrative USD 229 and USD 458 ten-year customer thresholds are conditional on stated landscape areas, water rates, achieved reductions, and controller settings and cannot be transferred to the Davis reference-crop benchmark.

### Gate

The 9,608.723-unit ceiling is the sum of the calendar-policy objective minus the perfect-weather reference-crop objective. It embeds a shortfall penalty of five and therefore is not a physical volume of water, crop loss, or welfare measure. It must remain in objective-equivalent units until an applicable crop coefficient, root-zone model, irrigation efficiency, system capacity, field area, yield response, and valuation are supplied. Comfort, reliability, crop welfare, and yield are not monetized.

## Discounting

`TR-CROSS-OMB-A94` is Level B methodological context. The 2026 Appendix C rates are 3.4%, 3.5%, 3.6%, 3.7%, 4.0%, and 4.1% nominal and 1.1%, 1.3%, 1.4%, 1.6%, 2.0%, and 2.0% real for 3-, 5-, 7-, 10-, 20-, and 30-year maturities. They are not applied because no case identifies compatible cash flows, useful life, project horizon, or jurisdictional valuation rule.

## Decision-relevant missing evidence

The next empirical step is not to choose a literature cost and subtract it. It is to obtain:

1. a defined deployment unit and incumbent installation;
2. an implementable non-oracle policy;
3. equipment, installation, commissioning, integration, training, and recurring operating costs;
4. observed error rates and consequences;
5. useful life, replacement, maintenance, and communication requirements;
6. safety, reliability, regulatory, and authorization constraints;
7. a compatible outcome and valuation unit;
8. an explicit horizon and sensitivity analysis.

Until those conditions are met, the appropriate governance result is **re-audit warranted where static evidence is material; redesign not established**.
